Poor Man’s Covered Call

AAPL · July 13–October 2, 2026 · Trades at 10:00 a.m. New York time

A longer-dated, in-the-money call replaces 100 shares. Selling weekly calls helps offset its cost. This diagonal spread uses less initial cash, while adding option decay, volatility sensitivity, and early-assignment risk.

Algorithm

  1. Buy the backing call once. On July 13 at 10:00, buy one January 15, 2027 call. Choose the largest $5 strike at or below 75% of the 09:59 stock print: $240. This expiry has 186 days remaining at entry. Keep it until the fixed end date.
  2. Sell a weekly call. Each Monday, use the 09:59 stock print to choose K = ceil(1.03 × S / 2.50) × 2.50. Sell one call at 10:00, expiring that Friday. Its strike must exceed the long strike and its expiry must come first. A Monday holiday is skipped.
  3. Check quotes before entry. Require valid bid/ask at the signal and execution, a positive bid, and a spread no larger than 20% of midpoint. Use the chosen strike or skip; never choose a substitute based on later prices.
  4. Close the short before expiration. Buy it back Friday at 10:00. Close earlier at 10:00 if the 09:59 short is in the money with ≤ $0.10 of time value, or before an announced ex-dividend date that precedes its expiry. Keep the long call. On October 2, cover any short and sell the long.

Account value through time

PMCC NAV = cash + long-call value − short-call value. Fully paid positions; no margin loan. A lower opening debit does not guarantee lower percentage losses. The long call can lose its entire premium. Actual broker approval, margin, and assignment funding requirements vary.

Interpretation

The PMCC combines bullish exposure from the long call with premium from selling weekly upside. If a short call becomes cheaper, buying it back retains part of that premium; a sharp rally can instead make the buyback expensive. A conventional covered call uses shares, which have no expiry and receive dividends. The PMCC uses less cash, but its backing call expires and can lose its premium. It can serve a moderately bullish view over a defined period when conserving cash matters. A stock covered call can suit an existing shareholder seeking premium income and willing to sell shares at the strike.

Stock-price sensitivity		100 × (long-call delta − short-call delta). Usually bullish at entry, with less share-equivalent exposure than the matched covered call. Time decay		Short-call decay helps; long-call decay hurts. Net theta can be positive or negative, so collecting weekly premiums does not guarantee a gain from time passing.Shares have no option time decay. The short call contributes positive theta, all else equal.

Volatility sensitivity		Long-call vega offsets short-call vega. A parallel rise in implied volatility helps if net vega is positive; the balance depends on both strikes and expiries.Negative vega from the short call. Higher implied volatility raises its buyback cost, holding the stock price and time fixed.
Gamma
How delta changes		Long gamma minus short gamma. Near the weekly strike, the short call’s gamma can dominate as expiry approaches, reducing net delta quickly during a rally.Shares have zero gamma; the short call makes net gamma negative. Upside participation falls as the stock rises toward and through the strike.

These are structural Greek exposures, not measured historical values. The backtest does not estimate Greeks or separate the long call’s gain into stock movement, time decay, and volatility effects. References: diagonal-call exposures, covered calls, delta, and gamma.

Data, sources, and how to reproduce

Signals use the bar ending 09:59; simulated orders use the quote available by 10:00. Bars are explicitly end-labeled and converted to America/New_York. No later bar is used. Missing entries are skipped; missing mandatory exits beyond the 60-second quote limit stop the run. Missing marks can only carry a prior quote, with age disclosed.

RICs come from a predefined strike grid, confirmed by quotes present at the signal time; this is not a complete historical chain. The long strike uses moneyness, not an observed delta. Dividend dates, amounts, and announcement dates come from LSEG. An announcement becomes usable the following day because its intraday timestamp is unavailable.
