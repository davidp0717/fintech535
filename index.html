Interpolating across empty cells on UUUU’s $0.50 strike grid can invent prices where no quote or trade was observed, because closely spaced strikes do not guarantee liquidity.

Next week I will use MID_PRICE as the closing mark, while remembering quotes may be stale, and TRDPRC_1 as evidence of a reported trade rather than a closing mark.

Data notes

Missing values stay missing; there is no interpolation or forward filling, and zero remains a valid price. The median uses only contracts with both prices on the selected date; no matched pairs produces N/A.

The denominator includes contracts from their first returned price through expiry, even on later days with neither field. It can miss listings before their first observation or contracts that returned no data; it is not a verified count of all listed series.

This is a sample of Friday expirations and strikes within UUUU’s high/low range, not the full options market. Empty or failed identifiers do not prove a contract never existed. Daily last-trade values cannot establish an intraday fill.

Python reads the local LSEG pickle and exports this website’s data snapshot. The browser does not need a live LSEG connection.

The expired RIC suffix uses A–L for both calls and puts, correcting the starter’s put suffix: LSEG explanation.
