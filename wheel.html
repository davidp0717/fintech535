Wheel Strategy

AAPL · May 4–October 30, 2026 · Trades at 10:00 a.m. New York time

Sell a cash-secured put. If assigned, own 100 shares and sell covered calls. When the shares are called away, return to selling puts.

Algorithm

  1. When holding cash: each Monday, use the 09:59 stock print to select a put with K = floor(0.99 × S / 2.50) × 2.50. Sell one at 10:00 only if existing cash covers 100 × K plus the fee. Reserve the full strike amount.
  2. When holding 100 shares: sell one call with K = ceil(1.01 × S / 2.50) × 2.50. The strike follows the current stock price and can be below acquisition cost. No uncovered calls or borrowing.
  3. Wait through expiry: use that week's last exchange session. At least $0.01 in the money at the closing stock print means modeled assignment: a put buys 100 shares at K; a call sells them at K. Otherwise the option expires. Resume next Monday. No discretionary closes, stop losses or rolls.
  4. Use only available information: require valid signal and execution quotes, positive bid, spread ≤20% of midpoint, and quote age ≤60 seconds. Skip an unavailable chosen contract. Closed Mondays are skipped; June 19 and July 3 expiries shift to Thursday. Rules were fixed before calculating results, with no parameter search.

The covered-call benchmark buys 100 shares when flat and writes the same weekly call rule. Buy and hold purchases 100 shares at the first 10:00 ask. These comparisons use the same dates and funding; the prior assignment pages retain their original backtests.

Account value through time

NAV = cash + stock value − short-option liability + dividend receivable. Put collateral is part of cash, not an expense. Free cash = cash − put collateral. Covered calls are secured by the 100 shares.

Interpretation

The wheel earns option premium while alternating between cash and shares. A conventional covered call starts with stock; the wheel first sells a put and can miss a rally while waiting for assignment. Once shares are acquired, both strategies sell calls and give up upside above the strike. The wheel fits a willingness to buy at the put strike and sell at the call strike, with sufficient cash reserved. Premium cushions losses but does not remove stock downside, and selling calls below acquisition cost can lock in a loss.

Data, sources, and how to reproduce

Option sales fill at the observed bid; purchases, if required, at the ask. Each option transaction costs $0.65. Midpoint and adverse fills (an extra 10% of spread beyond bid/ask) rerun the same entries. These are simulated prices from one-minute, end-labeled bars; available size and actual executions are not observed. Stock transactions and assignments have no assumed fee.

Quote evidence: midpoint versus reported trade

Only observations with valid bid/ask, a trade field and positive reported bar volume are included, for the contracts actually sold from entry through expiry. A high R² shows close price correspondence, not that midpoint orders would execute. The last trade and quote need not occur simultaneously; bid fills and the cost sensitivity remain the main evidence for the execution assumption.

Volatility and Sharpe annualize daily returns using 252 sessions. This six-month sample does not establish long-run performance. A further test would use a separate bear or sideways period and interest on collateral at rates known at each date.

The saved observations come from LSEG Workspace: one-minute stock and weekly-option quotes, raw daily stock closes, and dividend events. Contract identifiers follow a predetermined $2.50 strike grid and are accepted only with signal-time observations; this is not a complete historical options chain. The rule specification was frozen on November 1 before performance was calculated, not before the historical trading window.

Shares held entering the May 11 and August 10 ex-dates earn $0.27 each, accrued then paid May 14 and August 13. This includes $54 in the buy-and-hold benchmark. Cash interest, taxes, market impact and settlement delays are excluded. The main assignment model uses expiry intrinsic value; contrary exercise and after-hours pin risk are not modeled.
